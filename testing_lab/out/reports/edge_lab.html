Where does BenPom beat Kalshi, and how sure can we be?

THE ANSWER — THERE IS NO EDGE. THE +34.7% WAS A BROKEN TIMESTAMP. The market price this study used, prob_a_t2h, is anchored two hours before the market closes — and these markets close when a winner is declared. So it is not a pre-match price: 74.3% of the sample was taken during the match, a median of 39 minutes after the first map started (§3). Priced honestly — pre-match only, paying the ask, paying Kalshi's fee — the whole thing returns +0.19%. Two further findings agree. (1) Backing every market underdog blindly, model switched off, returned +32.55% against the model's +34.71% — the model is worth about +2.2 points on top of ignoring it, and it is slightly worse at picking winners (59.7% vs 60.1%). (2) This exact thesis traded live for a week in July: 719 fills, $308.69 lost, and a bootstrap says that is real underperformance, not variance (p = 0.000). The backtest edge did not survive contact with a real order book.
Correction. An earlier version of this page, and the v10 lab's §9, described prob_a_t2h as a clean pre-match price and reported +34.71% on that basis. That description was wrong and the number is not usable. The corrected read is §3. The live-trading result (§4) was already pointing the same way.

1The question, and the control that answers it

The v10 lab reported +34.71% ROI at a ≥5c edge and flagged that 178 of 196 bets were on the underdog. That pattern has two possible explanations, and they have completely different consequences:

  1. The model finds mispriced underdogs. Real edge, worth trading.
  2. Underdogs were simply cheap. A market-wide bias any coin could have harvested; the model is incidental.

These are trivially separable, and the separating test is the one the original read was missing: back every underdog at the market price with the model switched off. If that returns roughly the same, hypothesis 2 wins.

Backing every favourite returned -14.03% (60.1% hit). That is the same fact stated from the other side: in this window the market's favourites were systematically overpriced. A model that is under-confident will disagree with those favourites by construction and look clever for doing so.

3The timestamp is coupled to the outcome

Kalshi VALORANT markets carry "This market will close and expire after a winner is declared". So close_time is not a schedule, it is the moment the match ended — and a price sampled at close − 2h is sampled two hours before the finish, not before the start.

Because match length varies, that sampling instant lands at wildly different points: 74.3% of prices were taken after the first map had begun, median +39 minutes in. And crucially, long matches are the ones underdogs win. The dataset therefore quotes a cheaper underdog price exactly when the underdog is likelier to win. That is not look-ahead in the ordinary sense — the sampling time is itself a function of the result.

The simulation that authorised those trades expected +$109. A common-random-number bootstrap over 4,000 resamples, unit = settled event, put the observed result at p = 0.000 under that hypothesis. The lab's own verdict: "real underperformance (p<0.05)" — the loss was not bad luck.

Why: adverse selection, not fees and not the spread

Fees were $0.00 (this series pays no maker fee), and the market-making markouts were fine — the spread was being captured. The damage is in which orders got filled:

This is the number that kills the backtest. A simulation buys at the quoted price on every match it likes. Reality fills you preferentially when you are wrong — 8.5c per contract of it here, against a mid-price backtest edge of 35% on an average 29c contract. The slippage curve in §8 models a fixed haircut; adverse selection is worse than a haircut, because it is correlated with the outcome.

5Is it just under-confidence? Mostly, yes

BenPom assigns the eventual winner an average probability of 0.5357; Kalshi assigns 0.5701. The model is systematically less sure of itself, so it disagrees toward the underdog on nearly every match — not because it has information, but because it hedges.

To test that directly, sharpen the model: scale its log-odds by ×2.525 so its average confidence exactly matches the market's, changing nothing about which team it prefers. Its ranking of matchups is untouched; only its certainty moves.

6Does it pick better underdogs?

This is the one place the model looks genuinely additive, and it deserves a careful read rather than a headline.

The underdogs it liked returned +42.76% against +16.79% for the ones it passed on — but note the hit rates run the other way (37.1% vs 46.7%). The model is not picking underdogs that win more often; it is picking longer-priced ones that pay more when they do. That is a price-selection effect, and with 75 matches in the comparison group it is nowhere near conclusive.

Against a fair-market null

If every contract were priced fairly, 196 bets of this size and shape would give an ROI standard deviation of 10.52%, so anything under +17.31% is indistinguishable from luck at this sample size. The observed +34.71% sits above that (one-sided p = 0.0005).

Read that carefully. It says the prices were not fair. It does not say the model is good — the blind-underdog control clears the same bar without any model at all.

Slippage

You never transact at the mid. Both lines survive 6c of adverse fill, and the gap between them barely moves — slippage does not change the conclusion, it just scales both down.

Any segment worth trading has to be named in advance and tested on data that did not suggest it.

10What would actually settle it

  1. Trade the control, not the model. If the thesis is "underdogs are underpriced", the honest instrument is a flat underdog rule. It returned +32.55% here and needs no model at all. Everything the model adds is inside the noise.
  2. Sharpen BenPom and re-measure. The ×2.525 scaling is a one-parameter fix that costs nothing and would make the probabilities honest. If a sharpened model still disagrees with the book profitably, that is a real signal; the current evidence says it mostly would not.
  3. Preregister and go forward. Fix the rule, the threshold and the stake before the next match, then score it prospectively. At 196 bets per three months, an effect the size of the model's marginal contribution (+2.2 pts) would need years to separate from noise — which is itself the answer about how much to size it.
Standing. This is a reporting exercise on a three-month window. Market data is never a fitting target and never a selection signal, nothing here was tuned, and nothing here promotes or demotes any model. The deployed model is unchanged.
