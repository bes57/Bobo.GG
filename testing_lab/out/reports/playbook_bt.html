THE ANSWER — DO NOT TRADE IT. The rule below, with its threshold picked on the first half of the window and scored on the second, returns -2.52% out-of-sample (p(profit) 0.433). It is profitable before costs and unprofitable after them. Even the optimistic maker version — you post the bid, you always get filled, fees are zero — is +2.95% with an interval from -25 to 33. There is no threshold on the curve that survives honest pricing.

1The rule

Two hours before a match, buy the side whose BenPom probability exceeds the Kalshi ask by at least 5 cents. One contract. Hold to settlement.

That is the whole strategy. No sizing ladder, no windows, no regional carve-outs — those all came from slicing a small sample and are how the previous playbook got its numbers. One threshold, chosen once, on data it was not scored on.

2What makes this backtest different

Three defects broke every previous read on this question. All are fixed here.

Both sides of every match are evaluated independently, so the rule never needs to know who won in order to decide whether to bet.

3Where the money goes

Same 84 bets, priced four ways. The rule clears a small profit at an untransactable mid, loses it to the spread, and goes negative on the fee. The fee is roughly 2c on a 30c ticket — 6–7% of stake.

The maker bound is the only positive column, and it is optimistic twice over: it assumes you are filled at your own bid on every bet you want, and it charges no fee. Real maker fills are the opposite of free — the live autopsy measured 8.5c per contract of adverse selection, which is larger than the entire +2.95% it shows here.

5What would make this tradeable

Not a different threshold — the curve has no hiding place. The three things that would actually change the answer, in order of size:

  1. Stop paying the spread and the fee. Quoting as a maker rather than lifting the ask is worth roughly 5 points here. That is the entire gap between a losing rule and a break-even one, which is why the existing deployment playbook is a maker strategy. It is also where adverse selection lives, so the gain is not free.
  2. A model that discriminates better. On this window BenPom picks the winner slightly less often than the market does. A rule built on it is trying to buy an information edge that the measurements do not show.
  3. More data. 253 matches over three months is not enough to resolve an effect of the size in question; the intervals here are ±30 points. This market has not existed for long enough to answer the question yet.
Standing. Reporting only. Market data is never a fitting target or a selection signal, and nothing here changes the deployed model or the trading configuration.
