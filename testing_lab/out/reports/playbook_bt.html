THE ANSWER — POSITIVE, BUT IT IS NOT THE MODEL'S EDGE. The rule below, threshold picked on the first half of the window and scored on the second, returns +13.14% out-of-sample (n=44, CI [-26, 55], p(profit) 0.733). But backing every market underdog with the model switched off returns almost the same, because this book carries a real favourite–longshot bias: a 65c favourite wins 58.5% of the time. The rule works because it is mostly a way of buying underdogs, and the interval is wide enough that a losing quarter is entirely ordinary.

1The rule

Two hours before a match, buy the side whose BenPom probability exceeds the Kalshi ask by at least 8 cents. One contract. Hold to settlement.

That is the whole strategy. No sizing ladder, no windows, no regional carve-outs — those all came from slicing a small sample and are how the previous playbook got its numbers. One threshold, chosen once, on data it was not scored on.

2What makes this backtest different

Three defects broke every previous read on this question. All are fixed here.

DefectPrevious readsHere
Price timestampT−2h from market close, and these markets close when a winner is declared — 74% of prices were sampled mid-match T−2h from the real scheduled start, rebuilt from raw candles; median offset −120 min, nothing post-start
Executiontransacted at the mid pays the ask, with the real book spread
Feesnone Kalshi taker fee ceil(0.07·C·P·(1−P)) on every ticket
Threshold choiceswept, best reported chosen on the first half, scored on the second

Both sides of every match are evaluated independently, so the rule never needs to know who won in order to decide whether to bet.

3Where the money goes

Same bets, priced four ways. The rule stays positive throughout, but the fee alone costs 6.4 points — roughly 2c on a 34c ticket, 6–7% of stake. Costs do not kill this rule; they just take a third of it.

The maker column is optimistic twice over: it assumes you are filled at your own bid on every bet you want, and it charges no fee. Real maker fills are the opposite of free — the live autopsy measured 8.5c per contract of adverse selection on a ~34c ticket, which is most of the margin shown here. That is the single biggest reason a backtest like this overstates what you would actually collect.

5What would make this tradeable

The rule is positive, so the question is whether the margin is real and collectable. Three things decide that, in order of size:

  1. Adverse selection, which this backtest cannot see. Quoting as a maker looks worth 15 points, but the live week measured 8.5c per contract of adverse fill — you get filled preferentially when you are wrong. That is correlated with the outcome, so no fixed haircut models it, and it is large enough to erase the margin.
  2. The model is barely involved. Backing every underdog with the model switched off returns almost the same as the rule. What is being harvested is the book's favourite–longshot bias — a 65c favourite wins 58.5% — not a BenPom insight. If that bias closes, the rule closes with it.
  3. More data. 253 matches over three months is not enough to resolve an effect of the size in question; the intervals here are ±30 points. This market has not existed for long enough to answer the question yet.
