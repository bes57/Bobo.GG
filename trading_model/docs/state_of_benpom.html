Round 8: the decay function, settled — and the 50-50 belt, resolved

Why exponential, and in what units

With games as the clock, every functional form was raced on identical inputs (v2 base, β refit on ≤2024, scored 2025–26):

Reversed asymmetry (losses remembered longer)0.64544clearly wrong direction — the sign is real
Discussion. Exponential is the right family, and games are the right clock: exponential decay is the unique memoryless forgetting rule, and the data says information arrives per game played, not per week elapsed — every attempt to reintroduce calendar time (envelopes, box windows, heavy tails that preserve old eras) scored worse or flat. The one refinement that beats plain exponential is asymmetric memory: losses fade ~1.7× faster than wins. A plausible reading: wins against rated opposition are hard evidence about a team's ceiling, while losses mix in noise (dead maps, experimentation, tilt). Both tested ratios agree, and the reversed sign is decisively worse.

The 50-50 belt

On the candidate surface the train-fit calibration slope is 0.9989 — globally there is nothing left to expand; the "too close to 50-50" defect was a symptom of the old ratings, not a missing calibration layer. Confirmations: slope-only Platt, odd-cubic recalibration, and rolling-12-month slope layers were all fit walk-forward and all lost or tied on test (the rolling layer keeps estimating slopes >1.25 from trailing data and still loses — trailing recalibration chases noise, same lesson as rolling β). A power-link that expands small rating gaps also failed. What remained was one band — 0.70–0.80 favorites winning ~80% — and the asymmetric decay closes half of it structurally (gap +0.069 → +0.053, ≈1.3σ residual: monitor, don't curve-fit). Against Kalshi, v3's favorite pricing now sits within ~1.5 points of the market in every band — the compression story is resolved; the market's remaining edge is post-break information, not sharpness.

Session 9: the margin-outcome proposal ("BenPom v3") — investigated to the bottom

The idea (user's): judge and train the model on average round margin per map instead of binary outcomes — FNC beating KC 2-1 with maps +2/+2/−7 should count as evidence FOR a KC-favored model, not against it. Margins carry more information per match than a coin-flip outcome, so fitting on them should sharpen the model, especially at high probabilities.

Why it fails — a real domain fact worth keeping: margins systematically understate win probability. The better team disproportionately wins the close maps (every overtime win has margin exactly 2), so any margin-derived probability under-rates favorites — the opposite of the intended sharpening. The empirical margin→win map is brutally steep (avg margin of just +1/map already implies 84%): in the FNC–KC example the margin ledger says KC "deserved" 84%, far more than the model's 53%. And the place margins genuinely help — estimating ratings — is already margin-native (the Massey target is the round margin, transform tuned in round 1). Margins for ratings, binary for calibration is the optimal division of labor, and it's what the champion already does.

Salvaged from the investigation:

  • The margin-MSE metric is adopted as a permanent secondary evaluation (exactly the user's scoring idea): predicted vs realized series avg margin/map. The champion beats production on it too (MSE 43.1 vs 45.4, correlation 0.148 vs 0.104) — the model is better in margin-space as well, independently confirming the binary result.
  • The premise check came back changed: "the model is scared of high probabilities" was true of production but is no longer true of the champion — at [0.7,0.8) it now prices favorites at 0.746 vs the market's 0.718 (n=24, favorites won 0.667), i.e. the model is now the bolder side. The timidity was cured by the games-decay/asym stack, not by margin outcomes.

Session 6: "same as Kalshi?" — no: the edge lives in the disagreements

Equal log-loss with an independent model is not "the same as Kalshi" — correlation between the two is only 0.78, and profit comes precisely from where they diverge. Simulated trading (buy BenPom's side at the pre-match market price — taker, worst case, no spread capture):

Divergence filternROI95% CI
All joined matches, any divergence168 +7.1% [-10%, +25%]
Divergence > 5 pts88 +30.9% [+4%, +58%] — excludes 0
Divergence > 10 pts37 +25.1% [-21%, +71%]

Hit rates sit below 50% by design — the model tends to take the cheap (underdog) side and wins more often than the price implies. The bot's maker-side execution (spread + rebates) would add to these taker-priced numbers.

How can log-loss tie while ROI is +31%? They're different functionals on different samples in different units. Log-loss parity is an average over all matches; trades exist only on the disagreement subset. And log-loss lives in probability space while ROI lives in price space: if the market prices an underdog at 20¢ and its true chance is 30%, the log-loss gain from knowing that is tiny (it only registers the 30% of the time the dog wins) — but the trade's expected ROI is +50% (buy at 0.20 what's worth 0.30). Conversely, when the model is the wrong one, log-loss punishes it logarithmically while the dollar loss is capped at the stake. Small probability edges on cheap contracts are worth little in log space and a lot in dollars.

Robustness of the +31% (n=88, |div|>5pts): drop the top 1/2/3/5 winning trades → +28% / +26% / +23% / +19% (not concentration-driven). By window: May/quals +37%, Stage 2+EWC +45%, London −21% (the known deficit window). By side: 79 underdog-side buys at +34% (hit 44%), 9 favorite-side at +15% (hit 67%). A 2¢-better maker fill scores ≈ the same (+30%); real taker slippage would shave a few points the other way.

Traded-surface package (native series veto-MC, n=999): overall-rating MC + opponent-aware pick scores (×(1.75−opp_win)) + a walk-forward pick-side bonus (picker's map logit +b_pick; the effect is real and growing — raw picker winrate 52.5%, b_pick ≈ +0.09 by 2026) scores 0.64497, best of seven variants. Per-map rating splits and the pick bonus double-count each other (picked maps are the good maps) — use the bonus, drop the splits (SHRINK_K→∞ on this surface).

London autopsy (the one remaining deficit window): the gap is NOT cross-region (model 0.722 vs market 0.718 there) — it's same-region rematches (n=7) plus CN sides that both model and market underpriced (CN won 50% at London, priced ~33-36%). Tested and rejected: a head-to-head/rematch term (prior winner rewins 53.2% vs 53.8% model-implied — ratings already capture it) and recency-weighted region offsets (unstable, hurt London). The residual is market-side LAN information; accepted and monitored.

Built: a prospective prediction logger (testing_lab/log_predictions.py, idempotent, appends candidate probs for every upcoming match — 71 logged today) so model-vs-market evidence accumulates forward without reconstruction. Automatable via cron when desired.

Where the market wins: the post-break window

Definition — "post-break": a match where at least one team is playing its first series after 45+ days without an official match — in this window, that's mostly teams returning from the ~10-week gap between the end of Stage 1 (late May) and the start of Stage 2 (mid July), plus teams that missed an event. Rosters, comps, and form shift over the break, but no official results exist yet — so BenPom's ratings are frozen on pre-break form while the market prices in scrim reports, roster news, and expectations.
